# algomonster alternatives: cheaper, video-first, and system-design-heavy options compared (plus when AlgoMonster is still the better buy)

Searching for AlgoMonster alternatives usually means one of four things. You want to spend less. You want video explanations instead of text. You want a bigger problem bank. Or you need system design coverage that AlgoMonster never really promised.

Those are four different problems, and they don't have the same answer. Someone who wants free YouTube walkthroughs and someone prepping for a senior system design loop shouldn't end up on the same platform. So instead of ranking platforms 1 through 7 and pretending that's useful, this compares the real alternatives by the bottleneck they solve, with the numbers third-party roundups actually publish. AlgoMonster shows up at the end, because for some of these bottlenecks it's the wrong tool and for one of them it's still the best value on the market.

## What AlgoMonster actually is, in one paragraph

Built by ex-Google engineers, it teaches 48 coding patterns rather than 3,000 problems. Each pattern comes with a conceptual explanation, a reusable code template, and a handful of problems that apply it. There are around 231 lessons and problems, roughly 700 interactive diagrams, decision flowcharts for figuring out which pattern a new problem needs, and a Speedrun mode for timed review. Everything is text and diagrams — no video anywhere, by design. Solutions are available in Python, Java, JavaScript, C++, Go, and Haskell. Add in an AI assistant, company question banks, a Foundation course, and a "Build an LLM" course bundled into the same subscription.

The curriculum is designed to be finished in 4 to 8 weeks. That's the pitch: compact, structured, no "which of these 3,000 problems should I do today" paralysis.

Now the limitations, because they're what drive people to search for alternatives in the first place.

| What you might want | Does AlgoMonster have it? |
| --- | --- |
| Video walkthroughs | No. Text and diagrams only |
| Huge problem bank for volume practice | No. ~231 problems vs. 3,000+ on LeetCode |
| Deep system design prep for senior roles | No. Introductory coverage only |
| Behavioral interview prep | Minimal |
| Live mock interviews or coaching | No. Self-paced only |
| Community discussion threads | Small compared with LeetCode |
| Monthly billing option | No. Annual or lifetime only |

If your bottleneck is any of the first six, an alternative is genuinely the right call. If your bottleneck is "I've done 200 random problems and still can't recognize patterns," keep reading, because switching away is probably a mistake.

## The alternatives, one at a time

### NeetCode: the best free starting point

A curated roadmap of LeetCode problems with video explanations, built by a former Google engineer. The NeetCode 75 and NeetCode 150 lists have become the default recommendation for people who want a short, defensible problem list instead of all 3,000. Most of the core content, including the videos, is free.

Choose it over AlgoMonster if you learn by watching someone talk through a solution, or if your budget is genuinely zero. Choose AlgoMonster instead if you want the patterns named, explained, and turned into reusable templates — NeetCode shows you solutions, but it's less explicit about the underlying pattern taxonomy. NeetCode Pro reportedly runs around $119/year or $219 lifetime.

### LeetCode: volume, company tags, and contests

Nobody beats LeetCode on problem count. Premium unlocks company tags, frequency data, and official solutions, and the contest system is a real pressure simulator that no course replicates. The trade-off is that LeetCode hands you a gym with every machine imaginable and no training program. If you already know what to study, that's fine. If you don't, you'll grind 200 problems and retain surprisingly little.

The clean combination most people end up with: patterns from one platform, volume from LeetCode. That's not a compromise, it's how the two products were designed.

### Educative: text-first learning plus actual system design

Educative covers DSA, system design, cloud, DevOps, and a lot more, with in-browser coding. The Grokking system design course has been the standard recommendation for senior-level prep for years, and it's the single strongest argument for Educative over AlgoMonster.

The catch is price and focus. Reported subscription pricing sits in the low hundreds to roughly $300 a year, and the catalogue is broad enough that it's easy to wander. If DSA is your only gap, you're paying for a lot of content you won't open.

### Design Gurus: patterns and system design in one place

The original Grokking coding interview courses teach around 16 patterns with visual explanations, and the platform bundles system design alongside them. It's the closest thing to "AlgoMonster's philosophy, but with the system design material included." Published prices vary widely by promotion and bundle, so check the checkout page rather than trusting a roundup.

### AlgoExpert: video quality, fewer problems

Around 160 curated problems with high-production video walkthroughs and an integrated coding workspace. Longer, more polished explanations than most alternatives. Fewer problems than AlgoMonster, and a subscription rather than a one-time payment. Reported annual pricing ranges from roughly $100 to $199 depending on which review you read, which tells you the price moves.

### Pramp and interviewing.io: the gap AlgoMonster doesn't fill at all

If your problem is that you freeze under observation, no amount of pattern study fixes it. Pramp offers free peer mock interviews; interviewing.io runs anonymous mock interviews with engineers from larger companies, with free access plus paid sessions. These are complements, not replacements — you still need something to study.

### GreatFrontEnd and the free path

If you're interviewing for front-end roles, GreatFrontEnd is aimed at your loop specifically. And if budget is the hard constraint, the Blind 75 or NeetCode 150 plus free YouTube explanations is a legitimate path. It costs nothing except the thing you were hoping to save: time and structure. That trade is real, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

## The other side of the ledger: what AlgoMonster still does better

Strip away the marketing and AlgoMonster's actual product is 48 named patterns with templates and decision flowcharts, on a curriculum you can finish in 4 to 8 weeks. No competitor in the list above sells exactly that. The patterns exist elsewhere, but usually as courses you buy separately, or as implicit knowledge you're supposed to absorb from grinding.

Two practical advantages that don't show up in feature grids: there's no monthly plan to forget about, and the lifetime option removes renewal anxiety entirely. Third-party comparisons consistently note the lifetime plan comes in below LeetCode Premium's annual price when a sale is running.

The 231-problem ceiling is real, though. Treat AlgoMonster as the first four to six weeks of a prep plan, then add volume practice elsewhere.

## Matching the tool to your real bottleneck

Work backwards from what's actually holding you up.

- **You can't recognize patterns on unseen problems.** Stay with AlgoMonster. This is precisely the problem the 48 patterns and decision flowcharts are built for, and it's the hardest one to solve with a problem bank alone.
- **You've learned the patterns and just need reps and speed.** LeetCode Premium, or free LeetCode plus a timed mock schedule. Don't buy another course.
- **You need video, or you won't do the reading.** NeetCode, free, and upgrade to Pro only if you hit the paywall.
- **System design carries real weight in your loop (typically senior roles).** Educative or Design Gurus for system design specifically. Keep your DSA platform separate and cheaper.
- **You freeze in live interviews.** Pramp or interviewing.io. This is a separate skill and separate money.
- **You have a front-end loop.** GreatFrontEnd, plus whatever pattern resource you already have.
- **You have four to eight weeks and no study plan.** AlgoMonster's structured 4 to 8 week path exists for exactly this constraint, and the free tier lets you test whether the format clicks before you pay anything.

## Stacking, not swapping

The most expensive mistake here is treating this as one decision. A workable pattern from people who document their prep publicly: pattern study first, volume practice second, mocks last.

Weeks one to five: learn the 48 patterns and internalise the templates. Weeks four to eight: run the NeetCode 150 or Blind 75 at speed on LeetCode. Weeks six onward: mock interviews, plus system design if your target level includes it. Add behavioral prep in the final two weeks.

The total isn't trivial, but it's a fraction of what any of these platforms cost individually at list price across three years, and it's ordered by dependency rather than by brand.

## FAQ

**Is there a free AlgoMonster alternative?**
NeetCode's core content is free, including the NeetCode 75 and 150 lists with video explanations. The Blind 75 plus free YouTube walkthroughs is also a legitimate zero-cost route. Neither gives you AlgoMonster's explicit pattern templates or the decision flowcharts.

**Does AlgoMonster have a monthly plan?**
No. The official plans are a limited free tier, Pro Yearly, and Pro Lifetime. If you specifically want month-to-month billing, LeetCode Premium at around $35/month is the usual answer.

**Is AlgoMonster worth it if I already pay for LeetCode Premium?**
If your gap is pattern recognition, yes — they solve different problems. AlgoMonster teaches the frameworks; LeetCode gives you the volume and company tags to apply them. Plenty of people run both and skip one of the more expensive alternatives instead.

**How does AlgoMonster's price compare with the alternatives?**
At the current promotional pricing, Pro Yearly is $99/year and Pro Lifetime is $189 one-time. NeetCode Pro is reported at roughly $119/year or $219 lifetime, and LeetCode Premium at roughly $159/year. Educative and AlgoExpert are reported higher. AlgoMonster's free tier is one of the narrower ones, which is the trade-off for its lower price.

**What's the biggest thing all of these alternatives miss?**
Live feedback. Every platform here is content you work through alone. If you've never done a timed interview with a stranger watching, budget for a mock interview service separately — it's the one purchase that no amount of course material substitutes for.

**Can I finish AlgoMonster in a month?**
The curriculum is built for roughly 4 to 8 weeks at one to two hours a day, faster if you already have some DSA exposure and slower on dynamic programming and graphs. The problem bank is deliberately thin after the curriculum ends, so plan your volume practice separately rather than expecting the same purchase to cover both.
